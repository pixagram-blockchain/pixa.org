Big Mac prices

PXS promises no price. Its feed refers to one Big Mac where each witness runs, priced in that country's own currency. These are the prices the feed reads, country by country.

Pick-up prices by country

The pick-up price is what the restaurant itself charges for the burger on its own: at the counter, at the kiosk or in the McDonald's app. Where several restaurants were read, low and high are the cheapest and the dearest, and the median is the price the feed uses.

Each price is shown in its country's currency.

Two prices for one burger

A delivery app such as Uber Eats keeps its own price list for the same restaurant, and it is usually higher than what the restaurant charges at pick-up. Choosing pick-up inside a delivery app does not change that: the app still shows its own list.

The feed reads the pick-up price only. The delivery price is kept in a second file and shown here so the difference stays visible.

latest.csvThe pick-up price per country. The feed reads its median column.
delivery.csvThe price delivery apps list, before their fees. For comparison only.

What goes in the file

  • The burger on its own, never the menu or meal.
  • The price a customer pays, in the country's own currency. No currency and no country is privileged.
  • A source someone else can check: McDonald's own price list or announcement, a count of restaurant prices that says how it was made, a press report of a restaurant price, a price a witness read in the McDonald's app or at the counter, with the place and the day, or, for a country nobody has read yet, The Economist's latest Big Mac index.
  • No row without a source. A country that is not listed has no pick-up price anyone could check yet, and no other country's price stands in for it.

Your country is missing, or its price is old

Read the price of a Big Mac on its own at your restaurant: in the McDonald's app, start an order to collect there. Then add it to the file. Several restaurants make a better row than one.

How the feed uses it

Each witness publishes how many PIXA the chain should count for one PXS, and the chain uses the median of those feeds. Starting with version 1.4.0, bigmac-feed takes the median of the witness's own country from latest.csv, divides it by PIXA's fair value in the same currency, and publishes the result. A witness on an earlier version still reads The Economist's index.

A witness whose country has no row states the price it pays with --bigmac-price. The feed never borrows another country's price.

Exchange rates on this page come from Frankfurter, which blends the reference rates central banks publish. They only convert the display; the files hold local prices and nothing else. Rows sourced to The Economist use its Big Mac index data, © The Economist, under CC BY 4.0.
